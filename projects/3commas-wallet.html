A Wallet From Zero

A non-custodial crypto wallet for 3Commas, the trading-bot platform, designed from an empty Figma page. The hard part wasn't the UI: it was making self-custody feel safe for someone buying crypto for the first time, without slowing down the pro who just wants to buy now. Research, task flows, 75 versions of one screen and the product that came out of it, in a 9-page comic.

The problem

  • No product structure at the start: first we had to decide what the wallet is and which jobs it does.
  • Crypto means responsibility: a wrong transfer or lost access can be costly.

The hard parts

  1. 01

    Three traders, one home screen

    ChallengeA beginner wants a first buy fast, an investor wants their own price in one tap, a pro wants zero friction. Each pulls the hierarchy a different way.

    MovePersonas set the order: the beginner gets the big Buy button, everyone gets Send, Receive and Buy in a floating bar one tap away.

  2. 02

    Self-custody without the fear

    ChallengeThe question I heard most: “what if I lose my coins?” The recovery phrase is the riskiest moment in a wallet and the one people most want to skip.

    Move18 screens just for create, back up, verify word by word and set a PIN. Safety copy in plain words, no jargon.

  3. 03

    Designing into a moving target

    ChallengeAn Android wallet was already in development, and a separate visual reference already existed. Drawing first would have meant redrawing later.

    MoveNo pixels before questions: five for the brief, three for the team. Change the look, merge the two, or redraw. Answered before the first frame.

  4. 04

    Trading-grade data on a phone

    ChallengePrice, change, periods, supported markets and volume, all for one coin, on a screen a first-timer can still read.

    MoveBorrowed a proven numpad sell pattern, then layered the chart and periods from 1H to ALL onto a single coin page.

  5. 05

    Exploring without drifting

    Challenge75 versions of the main screen: light, dark, blue, teal, cards, lists, charts. Easy to get lost in taste.

    MoveGrey wireframes first, so flow was fixed while it was cheap. Every discarded sketch had to answer a question.
